SECTION: Preparing
------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — TITLE
Allow the material to relax

STEP 1 — BODY
Unroll the molding so it can return to its original shape. This is a great time to verify your profile and application.

STEP 2 — TITLE
Optimize flexibility

STEP 2 — BODY
Warm the material to 80°F for maximum flexibility. To prevent damage, never use an open flame to heat the moulding.

STEP 3 — TITLE
Clean the moulding

STEP 3 — BODY
Wipe down the material with mineral spirits or soap and water to remove any dust prior to installation.

STEP 4 — TITLE
Prepare a smooth surface

STEP 4 — BODY
Ensure your mounting area is completely flat. Because the molding is flexible, it will mirror any underlying bumps or dips.

STEP 5 — TITLE
Wait to apply finishes

STEP 5 — BODY
Always paint or stain after installation. Flexing the molding while installing will crack and fracture a pre-applied finish.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION: Machining
------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — TITLE
Use standard woodworking tools

STEP 1 — BODY
Treat flexible moulding nearly the same as wood. It can be easily cut, sanded, and shaped using your typical shop equipment.

STEP 2 — TITLE
Secure the material

STEP 2 — BODY
Hold the moulding firmly against your table or fence when cutting to maintain control and ensure a clean, accurate line.

STEP 3 — TITLE
Make shallow passes with shaping tools

STEP 3 — BODY
If shaping the profile with a router, take several shallow passes rather than attempting one deep cut to achieve the smoothest result.

STEP 4 — TITLE
Lower your blade speeds when able

STEP 4 — BODY
Operate saws and routers at slower blade speeds, best for cutting materials cleanly without excessive heat buildup.

STEP 5 — TITLE
Protect stain-grade finishes

STEP 5 — BODY
Never sand or shape stain-grade moulding as this will permanently remove the surface-level wood grain pattern.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION: Installing
------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — TITLE
Plan your layout

STEP 1 — BODY
Pre-determine the exact material placement and layout before applying any adhesive or fasteners.

STEP 2 — TITLE
Install triangular blocking for floating crown

STEP 2 — BODY
When crown is mounted only to the wall, with no ceiling support, install wooden triangular blocking behind the moulding every 4-8 inches for continuous backing and a secure install.

STEP 3 — TITLE
Select and apply the proper adhesive

STEP 3 — BODY
Use an adhesive compatible with polyurethane and non-porous materials, such as PL Premium Max. Never standard wood glue. Apply adhesive to the moulding ends so joints stay tight through seasonal movement, and to the back face where the moulding meets the wall or substrate.

STEP 4 — TITLE
Fasten the material

STEP 4 — BODY
Although pin nails are the best as they recess cleanly into the piece, trim screws, finish nails, and brad nails are all acceptable fasteners. Pin nails should always be used on small profiles, or when nailing close to the edge is absolutely necessary. To reduce the chance of dimpling, lower the pressure on mechanical nail guns, set nails by hand, and pre-drill/pre-countersink trim screw holes. Never use staples as they will create linear dimples.

STEP 5 — TITLE
Minimize nailing

STEP 5 — BODY
Position fasteners away from the edges to prevent the material from bumping or tearing, and let the adhesive do the work.

STEP 6 — TITLE
Avoid excessive force

STEP 6 — BODY
Never stress the moulding into place. Material that is ordered to the correct specifications should install easily and naturally.

------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION: Finishing
------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — TITLE
Fill and caulk freely

STEP 1 — BODY
We recommend bondo-like fillers that are compatible with nonporous resin-based products for nail holes and your favorite caulk for seams. Standard products work perfectly with Flex Trim™.

STEP 2 — TITLE
Clean and install first

STEP 2 — BODY
Always install the moulding and thoroughly clean the surface before applying any paint or stain to ensure a flawless finish.

STEP 3 — TITLE
Painting Flex Trim™

STEP 3 — BODY
A primer coat is recommended, but not required. Any interior/exterior paint (other than pre-catalyzed epoxy) works great.

STEP 4 — TITLE
Staining Flex Trim™ (gel only)

STEP 4 — BODY
Since the grain is only on the surface, our product requires a gel stain. Remember to never sand stain-grade material, as it will permanently remove the surface wood grain. Apply clearcoat after staining.

STEP 5 — TITLE
Additional staining tips

STEP 5 — BODY
To achieve a darker color, apply the stain generously and work the pigment down into the recesses of the embossed grain. Wipe the excess stain with a soft cloth. Use a dry, soft brush to feather or remove any cloth marks for a blemish free finish, then let it cure completely. Gel stain on resin takes longer to dry than on wood and it must be completely dry to the touch, without any tackiness, before you move on. If the first coat isn't dark enough, apply a second, very thin coat of gel stain using the same process as the first coat. Because the first coat sealed the surface, this second layer will glide over the top, deepening the color without disturbing the layer underneath. Repeat if needed. You can also play with 2 different pigments: darker then lighter vs lighter then darker.
